September 20, 2014 – August 31, 2026

Scout was born on September 20th, 2014, in a little town east of Atlanta, Georgia. He came into my life just two months later, when my then-girlfriend and I decided to get a Golden Retriever. Raising a puppy that young definitely came with its challenges, but looking back, I'm so grateful I got to be there for the very beginning of his life. Even more than that, I'm grateful he got to be such a big part of mine.

From the moment Scout came home, I was never really alone. For nearly twelve years, he was my constant companion, always there beside me through the different chapters of my life. As he got older, he even accompanied me on multiple trips across the United States, sharing in adventures I know I'll always remember. It's hard to put into words how much it hurts to know he's gone, but I can take comfort in knowing that he lived a full life, filled with frisbees, long walks, treats, adventures, and, above all, so much love.

Scout discovered his love for playing fetch pretty early on, thanks to a spur-of-the-moment decision to buy him an ultimate frisbee. We were visiting relatives at my family's farm in Spring Hill, Kansas, and I started throwing it out into the field behind the house. He took to it almost immediately. Before long, the word frisbee had become permanently etched into his vocabulary, and it quickly became his favorite thing in the world.

As he grew up, his obsession with frisbees only got stronger. Whenever we'd visit my parents' house, he'd make it very clear that he wanted to go outside and play. He'd spend hours running back and forth across their property, from the backyard all the way to the cul-de-sac out front, chasing frisbees until he was completely worn out. Honestly, if it had been up to him, I think he would have played until he physically couldn't anymore.

Swimming was another one of his great loves. We made countless trips to parks along the Chattahoochee River, bringing along a water frisbee or, if we didn't have one, a good old stick. He'd happily run in and out of the water, chasing whatever I threw for him, never seeming to get tired of it. Those simple afternoons became some of my favorite memories with him.

As he got older, he developed another funny little obsession: dunking his entire head underwater to retrieve whatever he could find on the bottom. Usually, it was a rock. And somehow, the bigger the rock, the better. He'd plunge his head underwater, come back up with his prize, carry it to shore, drop it on the ground, and go right back for another one. He'd repeat the process over and over until he had accumulated a little collection of rocks along the water's edge. It was such a uniquely Scout thing to do, and it's one of those little quirks I'll always remember about him.

Sadly, toward the end of August 2026, we discovered that Scout had cancer. He suddenly lost the ability to walk with his hind legs one day, although that particular episode turned out to be unrelated to the cancer itself. Thankfully, he regained his ability to walk fairly quickly, and for a little while, things seemed to be looking up. But just a week later, on August 31st, he passed away rather suddenly.

I don't think you can ever truly prepare yourself for losing a companion who's been beside you for almost twelve years. Scout was there through so much of my life, and now I'm left trying to imagine what the days ahead will look like without him. There are so many little things I'll miss, so many familiar routines that won't be the same, and so many memories that I wish I could go back and experience just one more time.

I have so many beautiful memories with you, Scout. You weren't just my dog. You were my best friend, my adventure buddy, and my constant companion for nearly twelve years. You brought so much happiness into my life, and I hope you knew just how deeply you were loved.

I don't believe in an afterlife in the traditional sense, but I can't help holding onto the hope that, whatever comes next, somehow we'll find each other again. Maybe that's wishful thinking, but I like to believe that a bond like ours doesn't simply disappear.

I'll carry you with me for the rest of my life, Scout. You left a mark on my heart that time will never erase, and no matter how many years pass, you'll always be my boy.

Rest well, my Scouty boy. Thank you for nearly twelve wonderful years. I love you, and I'll miss you always.
