September 20, 2014 - August 31, 2026

(The day we got him[01_scout_baby.jpg 27%])

Scout was born on September 20th, 2014, in a little town east of Atlanta, Georgia. He came into my life just two months later, when my then-girlfriend and I decided to get a Golden Retriever. We settled on Scout after watching Disney's Bears, which had been released earlier that year and featured a baby grizzly bear who shared his name.

Raising a puppy that young definitely came with its challenges, but looking back, I'm so grateful I got to be there for the very beginning of his life. Even more than that, I'm grateful he got to be such a big part of mine.

From the moment Scout came home, I was never really alone. For nearly twelve years, he was my constant companion, always there ([20150116_080135.jpg 21% print]) beside me through the different chapters of my life. As he got older, he even accompanied me on multiple trips across the United States, sharing in adventures I'll always remember. It's hard to put into words how much it hurts to know he's gone, but I can take comfort in knowing that he lived a full life, filled with frisbees, long walks, treats, adventures, and, above all, so much love.

Scout discovered his love for playing fetch pretty early on, thanks to a spur-of-the-moment (Scout with his first frisbee[first_frisbee.jpg 58% mat]) decision to buy him an ultimate frisbee. We were visiting relatives at my family's farm in Spring Hill, Kansas, and I started throwing it out into the field behind the house. He took to it almost immediately. Before long, the word frisbee had become permanently etched into his vocabulary, and it quickly became his favorite thing in the world.

As he grew up, his obsession with frisbees only got stronger. Whenever we'd visit my parents' house, he'd make it very clear that he wanted to go outside and play. He'd spend hours running back and forth across their property, from the sloped, hilly backyard all the way to the cul-de-sac out front, chasing frisbees until he was completely worn out. Honestly, if it had been up to him, I think he would have played until he passed out from exhaustion!

When he'd get worn out and thirsty, he'd head over to a bucket my parents kept underneath the hose faucet in the front yard. He'd stick his frisbee in the bucket and start gulping down water. Once he'd had enough and was ready to go back to playing, he'd start putting his paw in the bucket, twirling the frisbee around and sloshing all the water out. I still don't understand what exactly compelled him to do that, but we spent many hot summer afternoons enjoying this silly little routine.

Swimming was another one of his favorite pastimes. We made countless trips to various parks along the Chattahoochee River, bringing along a water frisbee or, if we didn't have one, a good old stick we'd find somewhere along the trail. He'd happily run in and out of the water, chasing whatever I threw for him, seemingly never getting tired of it. Those simple afternoons became some of my favorite memories with him.

Scout would also frequently accompany me on runs along the Chattahoochee, provided there were enough spots along the way where he could get into the water and cool off if it was too hot outside. He absolutely loved coming with me, and he loved our stops by the river even more, since that meant he got to play fetch for a few throws before we continued on our way. It was a nice way for us to spend time together, and I always enjoyed having him along for the ride.

Another interesting activity he developed a liking for was dunking his entire head underwater to retrieve whatever he could find on the bottom. Usually, it was a rock, but really, anything submerged was fair game. The bigger the object, the better. He'd plunge his head underwater, come back up with whatever he found, carry it to shore, drop it on the ground, and go right back for another one. He'd repeat the process over and over until he'd accumulated a little collection of rocks and other miscellaneous items along the water's edge, typically in one big pile. I always found it funny how determined he was to collect these things, as if he had some sort of important job to do.

I also remember how much he loved chasing his tail when he was younger. I didn't really have a problem with it, other than worrying he might hurt himself or knock something over, so I eventually taught him that chasing his tail was something he shouldn't do. Of course, that didn't actually stop him. I remember he'd start sneaking into other rooms just so he could chase his tail without being seen. I even caught him doing it once on the cameras at his puppy playgroup, where I'd drop him off every once in a while while I was at work. There he was, in the middle of a big room full of other dogs playing with each other, and he'd apparently decided it was the perfect opportunity to chase his tail!

Scout also absolutely loved sitting on any seat that could fit more than just him. Whether it was a couch, a big bench, or even a chair he could somehow squeeze himself onto beside you, he wanted to be right there with everyone else. My parents have a swing on their back porch, and as soon as someone would sit down on it, if Scout knew about it, he'd be right up there with them, swinging back and forth and just enjoying his time.

I know a lot of people don't let their dogs get up on the furniture, but for some reason, I never really felt compelled to teach Scout that it wasn't okay. I just wanted him to feel like he belonged. And while it may have cost me a couch or two over the years, I feel like it was worth it to have a companion who really felt like part of the pack. He may have been a dog, but in his head, he was one of us. He was a person, and I always tried to treat him with the utmost respect.

Sadly, toward the end of August 2026, we discovered that Scout had cancer. We took him to the vet after he suddenly lost the ability to walk with his hind legs one day. They ran some tests and found that, while his legs themselves weren't the underlying problem, he unfortunately had cancer affecting his lungs. He regained his ability to walk the day after that visit, but his overall condition quickly deteriorated, and he passed away rather suddenly only a week later, on August 31st, 2026, less than a month shy of his 12th birthday.

I don't think you can ever truly prepare yourself for losing a companion who's been beside you for almost twelve years. Scout was there through so much of my life, and it's difficult to imagine what things will look like without him. There are so many little things I'll miss, so many familiar routines that won't be the same, and so many memories that I wish I could go back and experience just one more time.

I have so many beautiful memories with you, Scout. You weren't just my dog. You were my best friend, my adventure buddy, and my constant companion for nearly twelve years. You brought so much happiness into my life, and I hope you knew just how deeply you were loved.

I'll carry you with me for the rest of my life, Scout. You left a mark on my heart that time will never erase, and no matter how many years pass, you'll always be my good buddy.

Rest well, my Scouty boy. Thank you for nearly twelve wonderful years. I love you, and I'll miss you always.
